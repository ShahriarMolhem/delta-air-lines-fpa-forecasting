# Executive Summary | Delta FP&A Operating Driver Case Study

**Shahriar Molhem** · BBA Finance Student, University of Georgia · Independent educational portfolio

## Business question
How can reported airline financial statements and operating statistics help us understand revenue, cost pressure, and the financial risks behind a three-year operating plan?

## Actual financial results — publicly filed GAAP
- Delta's FY2025 consolidated revenue was **$63.364bn**, compared with **$61.643bn** in FY2024 (+2.8%).
- Reported operating income **declined $173m**, from $5.995bn to $5.822bn.
- Salaries and related costs increased by **$1.359bn**; aircraft fuel expense decreased by **$747m**, despite higher reported gallons consumed.
- A GAAP operating-income bridge reconciles FY2024 to FY2025 exactly, but does **not** establish business causation.

## Illustrative operating plan — not issuer guidance
Using FY2025 as the historical base, an **independent, hypothetical** FY2026–FY2028 driver-based model assumes modest capacity and passenger-unit-revenue growth alongside fuel and unit labor/other-cost inflation.

In the *student base case*, FY2026 operating revenue is modeled at **$65.638bn** and operating income at **$5.257bn** (8.0% margin). This is a **what-if cost-pressure illustration**, not a real Delta budget or a prediction of actual FY2026 results.

A 10% increase in modeled fuel expense per gallon lowers hypothetical 2026 operating income by approximately **$1.006bn**, with all other inputs fixed. This is a scenario sensitivity, not a probability forecast.

## Limitations
FY2021 reflects pandemic recovery and government grant recognition. Delta's other revenue includes ancillary/refinery businesses and the model groups some costs into a single ASM-dependent residual. CASM-Ex is reported non-GAAP data and is not treated as that residual. No internal Delta financial information was used.
